AI Insight Prompt Design

How the AI insights in a TikTok Studio redesign concept are prompted, checked, adapted to other markets and reviewed.

An independent design concept, not affiliated with or endorsed by TikTok. All creator data is seeded for the prototype. Prompt pack version 2026-11-28.11 · updated 28 November 2026.

1. The problem

Studio shows creators charts: views, likes, shares, how they compare. Most creators are not analysts. The insight line above each chart has one job: tell a creator what the chart means for them and what to try next, in words they already use.

The rules every card insight follows:

  • Pick the one to three findings that matter most and connect them; don't recap the chart.
  • No analytics terms (rate, ratio, median, percentile, per view) and no score numbers. When a number helps, write it the way people say it: "1 in 3", "almost 2×", "1/2".
  • A short paragraph of 2–3 sentences, at most 3 lines on the phone, then 1–2 one-line bullets; the last bullet is a concrete next step.
  • Claims about results are hedged ("may help"); next steps are direct. Nothing about how recommendations work.

2. Options considered

OptionWhy not, or why
Fixed templates onlySafe, but can't connect findings ("reach is up while fewer viewers liked it, because one post carried the week"). Kept as the fallback.
Give the model the raw analyticsIn an earlier analytics-insight project, handing the model raw tables led to mismatched time windows and trend lines that weren't in the data. The numbers have to be right before the model sees them.
Chosen: code computes, the model words it, code checks itCode owns every number and judgement; the model chooses what matters and says it plainly; code rejects anything that breaks the rules.

3. How it works

Studio data→Code: baselines, ranks, judgements→Model writes the line→Code checks→Screen

Code compares each area with a typical similar creator, words the comparison ("ahead of 6 in 10 similar creators"), and pre-computes the judgements a line can use, such as "one post brought 1/2 of the views but only 1/3 of the likes". A line that fails a check goes back to the model with the errors, once (twice if the only problem was length). If it still fails, a fixed template line is shown. (The Assistant first tries one narrow code repair: it swaps out a follow-up question its answer already covered.)

4. Worked example: learning from another creator's post

The creator opens a bigger travel creator's post. Four bars compare it with the creator's own typical post over the same 22 days since posting. What code hands the model:

BarThat postYour typical postCode's note
Views860.0K3.0Kleft out of the comparison: the account has 146.9K followers
Likes per 1K views71.975.1not ahead
Comments per 1K views3.82.2largest lead, "almost 2× as often"
Favorites per 1K views17.112.4ahead

Also in the data: its comment topics (asking for the itinerary, 1 in 3 comments; budget questions, 1 in 4; wanting a part 2, 1 in 5), the creator's own post whose top comment word is also "itinerary" (Amalfi by ferry), and when the creator's viewers are most active (Thursday around 6 PM).

Model output, unedited, passed every check on the first try (module other_post 1.8.0):

People comment on @budgetroutes' post almost 2× as often as on yours. 1 in 3 of its comments ask for the itinerary.

• Itinerary also tops your Amalfi post.
• Post your Amalfi route Thursday 6 PM.

What it chose and skipped: it ignored the 860K views (a bigger account, not a lesson), didn't claim likes, explained the comment lead with what people asked for, tied that to the creator's own post with the same top word, and turned it into one post with a day and time.

5. Checks in code

  • Grounding: every numeral in the line must appear in the data. This is a loose check (a substring match); spelled-out numbers are handled by the wording rules. Post ids and links never appear.
  • Plain words: per-language lists of analytics terms are rejected, as are score numbers next to an area name.
  • Plain numbers on post cards: no percent signs, "N times" or decimal multiples.
  • Same words as the chart: account cards say "similar creators", post cards "similar videos", matching each chart's legend.
  • Fits the screen: on a 393 pt-wide iPhone one row of the paragraph holds about 46 characters, so the 2–3 sentence paragraph gets up to 138; a one-line bullet gets 45 on a full-width card and 38 in the narrower bottom sheet. Limits count characters, so they approximate rendered width.
  • No assumed gender: the prompt asks for this in every language; code checks Portuguese and Indonesian.
  • Prompt injection: comments and titles arrive as <untrusted_text> and are filtered first. The tag lowers the risk; the output checks are the real control. A test case puts an instruction inside a comment.

6. Other languages and markets

The model writes in the creator's language rather than translating from English. Fewer characters fit in a phone line in some languages, so limits scale with the line capacity measured for each:

These settings come from desk research and need policy and legal review before any launch. Where the Assistant is off, every card has to make sense on its own.

Moderation has two lines. Code runs first: it undoes spelling tricks (t1gr1nho, s l o t, gacoooor), matches local slang ("bet", "tigrinho" and "plataforma pagando" in Brazil; "bet" counts only there) and drops comments with phone numbers, emails, @handles or links. The model is the second line and flags what slips through. A smoke test of 27 cases across four locales (US and UK English, Portuguese, Indonesian) (pack 2026-11-27.3, 3 runs per case) passed 27; 12 of 66 model calls needed a retry and none fell back to a template.

An example written into the Assistant prompt, for a blocked request in Brazil:

Creator: Vale a pena divulgar casa de apostas pra ganhar mais?
Assistant: Não ajudo a divulgar apostas, que são proibidas no Brasil.
 • Posso mostrar quais posts seus trazem mais seguidores.
 • Posso sugerir temas próximos ao que seu público assiste.
 • Em publi de marcas, use o selo de parceria paga ou #publi.

Assistant answers use 3–4 bullets. The example reflects a provisional 2026 rule on betting in Brazil; its wording would be re-checked before launch.

7. Review by a "new creator"

Code checks format and sources, not whether a creator would understand a line and act on it. So lines are also reviewed by two separate model sessions. This is done by hand before copy goes into the prototype; it is not scripted yet.

Fact checkerNew creator
Seesthe line and all the dataonly the card title, the chart described in words, and the line
Judgesis each claim supported, overstated or against the chart?what it says, what I'd do, what lost me, what I don't believe

The new creator must not see the data: a reviewer who does fills in what the line leaves out. Its prompt:

You are a TikTok creator who posts travel videos and has about 20K followers. You started opening TikTok Studio a few weeks ago. You know views, likes, comments, shares and followers. You do not know analytics words such as rate, percentile, median, baseline or engagement, and you have never seen how this app scores you.

You are looking at one card on your phone. You see the card title, what the chart under it shows, and the AI summary above the chart. You do not see any data behind it.

Read the summary once, the way you would between other tasks. Then answer honestly, as yourself:
1. understood: in your own words, what is it telling you? One sentence.
2. what_i_would_do: what would you do after reading it? One sentence, or "nothing" if it gives you nothing to do.
3. confusing: words or numbers you did not understand or had to read twice. Quote them, or [].
4. doubts: anything that seems to disagree with the chart, or a promise you do not believe. Quote it, or [].
5. would_act: how likely you are to act on it this week, 1 to 5.
6. one_change: one change that would make it more useful to you.

Do not judge grammar or style. Do not guess what the designers meant. If something is unclear, say so.
Return only a JSON object with those six keys.

A line passes when its meaning survives the paraphrase, nothing confused the reader, nothing seemed to contradict the chart, and they would likely act (4 or 5).

What it found on the Performance summary

Five versions of the account summary, written or edited by hand, were reviewed, one new-creator session each. None passed. Two scored 4: one said shares were "falling behind" while the chart showed them about level, the other used "Creator Rewards" without explaining it. A version whose facts were all correct scored 3, because "at 12 seconds it earned no Creator Rewards" seemed to contradict the chart, where Rewards read "ahead".

The cause was the chart, not the wording: the Rewards area adds gifts to Creator Rewards, so the two stories can't agree in three lines. The fix is a design change, showing gifts and Creator Rewards apart in the Rewards detail. Words like "ahead" and "about level" on the chart also confused the reader; those are interface labels, so they go to design rather than back to the model.

8. How it would be measured

  • Primary: how often an insight is followed by the step it suggests, for example starting a post from the card, or posting on the suggested topic within 7 days.
  • Guardrails: thumbs-down rate on insights, the share of lines shown from the template fallback, and regular human spot checks for unsupported claims.
  • Before launch: calibrate the new-creator review against interviews with real new creators on the same lines.

9. Limits

  • The data is seeded for the prototype; nothing here has run on real creator accounts.
  • The reviews are small: each version was reviewed once, by one persona, and the fact checker is the same model family that writes the lines. A real evaluation needs repeated runs, a judge from another model family and a set labelled by people.
  • Portuguese and Indonesian copy was judged by models, not native speakers. Market settings need policy and legal review.
  • Cost and latency are not measured: each line is one large-model call, with up to two retries.

Terms

Similar creators
In the prototype: 120 US travel creators with 10K–50K followers. On the Performance summary each area is scored 0–100 against them, where a typical similar creator scores 50; 41–59 reads as "about level".
Creator Assistant
The chat inside Studio where a creator can ask follow-up questions about their numbers.
Bottom sheet
The panel that slides up over a screen; its text column is narrower than a full-width card.
Template fallback
A fixed, pre-written line shown when the model's line fails the checks.
